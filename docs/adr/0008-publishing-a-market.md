# ADR 0008: Publishing is its own endpoint, from SUBMITTED only, and one way

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-15
- **Affects:** [1.3] #3, [1.4] #4, [BE][X] #62, [FE][1.1] #45, [2.1] #5
- **Implemented in:** `backend/market_service/service/market_service.py` (`publish`), `controller/routes.py`

## Context

ADR 0004 built two of three market states and left the third to this ticket. It
recorded the shape of the gap and none of the mechanics:

> `submitted` means complete and internally consistent, not live. Publishing
> stays [1.3] #3's job, and the audit-log requirement stays with it.

Four things were genuinely open, and the issue text does not settle them.

**The story says "publish a draft", the ADR 0004 table says `submitted`.** Read
literally, [1.3] #3 lets an administrator go from a half-typed form straight to
a market traders can bet on. Read against the table, `submitted` is the state
publishing starts from.

**`POST /markets` already exists and already takes a status.** Adding `open` to
the values it accepts is the smallest possible change, and `MarketStatus` is
one enum, so the field accepted it the moment the member was added.

**"Publish blocked unless all required fields are present" duplicates a rule
that already exists.** `service/validation.py` was written as a pure function
over an entity precisely so this ticket could reuse it, and ADR 0004 says so.

**The acceptance criteria end somewhere this ticket cannot reach.** "Market
appears in trader browse" needs a trader browse, and that is [BE][X] #62,
assigned to someone else and scoped as "list, detail, search/filter".

## Decision

**Publishing is `POST /markets/{id}/publish`, and it carries no body.**

**Only a SUBMITTED market may be published.** A draft is a `409`
(`market_not_submitted`) telling the administrator to submit it first.

**Every submission rule re-runs at publish time, against the clock now.** A
failure is the same `draft_incomplete` `422`, with the same `details` list, and
a different sentence.

**Publication is one way.** A published market is `409`
(`market_already_open`) to a second publish and to every later save, autosave
and resubmission alike.

**`status` on `POST /markets` is narrowed to `draft | submitted`.** It is typed
`Literal[MarketStatus.DRAFT, MarketStatus.SUBMITTED]` rather than the whole
enum, so `open` is a `422` and `/docs` lists the two values the form may send.

**This ticket ends at the status.** `MarketStatus.OPEN` is the entire contract
with [BE][X] #62: the browse query is `status == MarketStatus.OPEN`, #62 is
built in this service and imports the member rather than retyping the string,
and `ix_market_markets_status` already indexes the column.

## Why a separate endpoint rather than `status: "open"`

**A publish that carries terms can change terms.** `POST /markets` is an upsert
over the whole document — that is what ADR 0004 decided and what makes autosave
work. A publish through it would arrive carrying a full set of terms and would
write them, so one request could alter a market and expose it to traders in the
same call. The audit entry would then record terms that had existed for
microseconds and had never passed through the submit button. A publish that
carries nothing cannot do that: what goes live is what was already submitted,
and the entry is true by construction.

**Idempotency on `draft_key` is the wrong identity here.** `draft_key` is
generated by the browser to name a form session, and its whole job is to make
repeated calls collapse into one row. Publication is a single decision about a
market the server has already named. Addressing it by `id` says that, and it
also means a publish cannot create anything — there is no row for
`POST /markets/{id}/publish` to insert.

**The states are no longer symmetric.** `draft` and `submitted` are two
outcomes of one form, so one endpoint for both is honest. `open` is a different
kind of event: it is the only transition that exposes anything to anyone, the
only one that is irreversible, and the only one an administrator will be asked
to account for later.

## Why from SUBMITTED only

**It is what ADR 0004 already decided,** and the story's "publish a draft" is
using "draft" the way people do — meaning an unpublished market — rather than
naming the state.

**A draft is the one state the autosave is still writing to.** Every three
seconds, from a form that is still open. Making that tradeable means traders
pricing against terms that an idle timer may overwrite before they finish
reading them.

**Collapsing the two makes `submitted` vestigial.** If publish re-validates
anyway, and it does, then `draft → open` is strictly quicker and nobody would
ever press submit. The state would survive only for people who chose the slow
path, which is not a state, it is a habit.

**Two decisions leave two audit entries.** `market.submitted` says these terms
are final; `market.published` says these terms are now in front of people who
will commit credits to them. A market can sit submitted for a week between
them. Folding them into one loses the question the log will actually be asked.

## Why validation runs again

Not defensiveness. A market is validated at submission against the clock at
submission, and `close_time` must be strictly in the future. Leave a submitted
market alone for long enough and that stops being true, so publishing it would
put a market in front of traders that has already closed.

`problems_blocking_submission` is a pure function of an entity and an injected
`now`, which is exactly the shape that makes a second call cheap and a second
definition unnecessary.

The error is deliberately the same `draft_incomplete` code with the same
`details` shape. The offending fields are the same fields, keyed the same way,
so a frontend that renders a refused submission renders a refused publish with
no new branch. Only the message changes, because the admin pressed a different
button.

## Consequences

**Two 409 codes, not one.** `market_not_submitted` and `market_already_open`
have different remedies — press submit, versus reload and stop offering the
button — so a single code with two messages would make the frontend parse
prose. `market_not_editable` stays as it was, for the autosave that arrives
after submission, because it offers a remedy (submit again) that a published
market does not have.

**`_save_once` gained a guard, and it is load-bearing.** An OPEN market refuses
every save regardless of the status the request asked for. Without it, the form
still open behind the publish button reverts a live market to a draft on its
next tick. This is the narrow version of what [1.4] #4 generalises.

**`published_at` is a new column and needs a hand-applied migration.**
`sql/migrations/0003-market-published-at.sql`. `create_all` reaches a fresh
database and never an existing one. The new `open` status needs no DDL at all:
the column is a non-native `Enum`, and SQLAlchemy has defaulted
`create_constraint` to False since 1.4, so there is no CHECK to widen.

**Publication stays with the creator.** `publish` reuses `get`, so another
administrator gets the same `404` they get reading the market, even though ADR
0007 makes the admin tier flat. Every other route in this service is already
scoped that way; widening it is a decision for a ticket that asks for one, not
a side effect of this one.

**The trader half of the second acceptance criterion is not here.** The status
moves and nothing yet reads it. That is the seam, not an omission: #62 owns the
unauthenticated route, the detail projection and the search and filter, and
building a second public list here would leave Ihsan to rebase onto it or throw
it away. Tick the criterion when #62 lands.

**There is no unpublish.** Nothing in epics 1 through 4 asks for one. [2.3] #7
closes a market early, with a reason, which is a forward transition to a state
this enum does not have yet. If a genuine "put it back" appears, it is a new
state and a new audit action, not a relaxation of the guard above.

## Alternatives rejected

**`status: "open"` on `POST /markets`.** One line, no new route, and it reuses
the guard and the session that are already there. Rejected on the first
argument above: an upsert that also publishes is a request that can change
terms and expose them in one call, and it would make the audit entry a record
of something that had never been submitted.

**`PATCH /markets/{id}` with a status field.** More REST-shaped, and it
generalises to [2.3] #7's close and epic 3's resolve. Rejected because a
general status-setting endpoint has to decide which transitions are legal from
a table anyway, and it invites a body that carries other fields. One endpoint
per transition is more code and states the legal moves in the route list.

**Idempotent republish — 200 and do nothing.** Kinder to a double-click.
Rejected because the two 409s carry information the frontend wants, and a
silent success on a market that was already open is indistinguishable from one
that just went live, which is the more dangerous reading of the two.

**Publishing without re-validating.** The market was checked at submission and
nothing between then and now can edit it — until [1.4] #4 allows resubmission,
at which point it can. And the clock moves on its own regardless.
